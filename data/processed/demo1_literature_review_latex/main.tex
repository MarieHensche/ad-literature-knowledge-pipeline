\begin{abstract}
The increasing prevalence of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) emphasizes the urgent need for innovative diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) have demonstrated considerable potential to improve understanding, detection, and treatment of this intricate condition. This narrative review synthesizes findings from 30 studies published between 2020 and 2024, focusing on AI applications in AD research. Key contributions include the development of predictive models employing machine learning, particularly in neuroimaging and assessments of cognitive decline. Several studies report high accuracy rates, with methods such as deep learning classification achieving up to 99\% accuracy in classifying dementia stages. However, significant limitations persist, including challenges related to dataset sizes, generalizability, and population diversity. Only a few studies thoroughly address these gaps, indicating a pressing need for more inclusive and robust research frameworks. This review underscores the vital role of AI in propelling AD research and stresses the necessity for future inquiries to overcome methodological constraints and enhance model applicability across varied settings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Alzheimer's disease (AD) represents a significant global health challenge, affecting millions of individuals and their families. The pursuit of early diagnosis and effective treatment remains critically important, as traditional methods often fail to detect the disease until it has progressed significantly. Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have opened new avenues for enhancing our understanding and management of AD. By leveraging AI techniques such as deep learning and machine learning, researchers can analyze extensive and complex datasets encompassing neuroimaging, genetic, and clinical information to identify novel biomarkers and improve diagnostic accuracy. This literature review aims to synthesize and critically orient existing studies published between 2020 and 2024 regarding the application of AI in Alzheimer's research. It will emphasize key themes such as predictive modeling, AI-assisted diagnostics, and the exploration of innovative therapeutic pathways. Through this synthesis, the review seeks to contextualize the current landscape, outlining how AI technologies can potentially transform Alzheimer's research and clinical management. It will also spotlight areas necessitating further investigation, particularly those involving diverse populations and the practicality of implementing AI solutions in clinical settings. Nonetheless, considerable challenges persist in the field, including model generalizability across varying demographics and complexities inherent to clinical application. As we synthesize findings, we aim to address these gaps and propose future directions for AI in AD research. The following sections will delve deeper into methodological advancements and their implications in clinical practice.

However, challenges concerning model generalizability across different populations persist, as highlighted in various studies advocating for more representative datasets \citep{ref_10_1016_s2589_7500_22_00169_8,ref_10_1016_j_ejrad_2023_110934}.

\subsection{Comparison of Approaches}

\textit{This section critically compares various methodologies applied in Alzheimer's disease research through artificial intelligence, discussing their unique strengths, limitations, and contexts of applicability.}

Recent studies have introduced diverse methodologies aimed at tackling the challenges posed by Alzheimer's disease. Deep learning approaches, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have gained traction for their ability to analyze complex neuroimaging data. For instance, the DEMNET model demonstrated high accuracy (95.23\%) in classifying different stages of Alzheimer's based on MRI images \citep{ref_10_1109_access_2021_3090474}. In contrast, conventional machine learning techniques often employ simpler feature extraction methods, which may lack the robustness of deep learning networks when dealing with volumetric data, as noted by advances achieved with CNNs \citep{ref_10_1016_j_mri_2021_02_001}. Furthermore, while transfer learning enhances predictive capabilities, it raises concerns regarding generalizability across diverse populations \citep{ref_10_3389_fpubh_2022_772592}. Multimodal frameworks like MADDi utilize clinical and imaging resources to improve diagnostic accuracy, achieving performance metrics exceeding 96\% \citep{ref_10_1093_jamia_ocac168}. However, limitations are prevalent, particularly concerning data diversity and cohort sizes, which can skew model performance \citep{ref_10_1002_alz_13390,ref_10_1016_j_ejrad_2023_110934}. The trade-offs between complexity and interpretability are critical; simpler models can provide clearer insights into decision-making processes but may sacrifice accuracy compared to sophisticated deep learning methodologies.

\section{Limitations and Research Outlook}

\subsection{Paper-Reported Limitations}

\textit{This section synthesizes the limitations explicitly reported by the included papers on AI applications in Alzheimer's disease. Recurring limitations include sensitivity to imputation strategies, data diversity, and generalizability, emphasizing the need for more robust models and sample size considerations.}

The exploration of artificial intelligence (AI) in Alzheimer's disease research has yielded various studies, each reporting limitations pertinent to their methodologies and outcomes. A common theme among these studies is the \textbf{sensitivity to the choice of imputation strategies} in handling missing data, which can significantly influence model performance \citep{ref_10_1016_j_neuroimage_2020_117203}. Furthermore, the \textbf{lack of diversity} in datasets has been highlighted as a pressing concern; specific studies indicate that the invasiveness and costs of biomarker collection pose challenges to wider applicability \citep{ref_10_1002_alz_13390}. Additionally, \textbf{class imbalance} within datasets has emerged as a notable limitation, affecting the sensitivity and specificity of existing diagnostic models \citep{ref_10_1109_access_2021_3090474}. Issues regarding model generalizability are frequently mentioned, with small sample sizes across various studies limiting external validation \citep{ref_10_1016_j_ejrad_2023_110934}. Other limitations noted universally among studies include difficulties related to overfitting due to inadequate sample sizes \citep{ref_10_3390_brainsci10020084} and constraints tied to conventional clinical diagnostic tools, which do not meet the demands for affordability and accessibility \citep{ref_10_1038_s41598_020_80312_2}. In summary, while advancements in AI for Alzheimer's research are notable, significant limitations underscore the need for further enhancement in robustness, model validation, and dataset diversity across future research endeavors.

\section{Conclusion}

\textit{This literature review highlights significant advancements and challenges in the application of artificial intelligence (AI) for Alzheimer's disease (AD) research, emphasizing various methodologies used in recent studies.}

The literature synthesis on AI applications in Alzheimer's disease reveals substantial advancements, particularly in predictive modeling and diagnostic accuracy. Most studies adopted machine learning techniques, with deep learning methods as the predominant approach \citep{ref_10_3389_fpubh_2022_853294,ref_10_1016_j_neuroimage_2020_117203,ref_10_3389_fpubh_2022_834032}. Notably, several approaches employed convolutional neural networks, achieving accuracy metrics that surpass traditional methodologies \citep{ref_10_1016_j_mri_2021_02_001,ref_10_3390_brainsci10020084}. Despite these advancements, significant limitations persist. Issues such as dataset diversity and model generalizability commonly hinder the applicability of findings across varied populations \citep{ref_10_1002_alz_13390,ref_10_1016_j_ejrad_2023_110934}. Furthermore, the sensitivity of models to preprocessing choices and class imbalances remains a recurring concern in the literature \citep{ref_10_1109_access_2021_3090474,ref_10_1016_s2589_7500_22_00169_8}. Future work should focus on enhancing model robustness through broader data collections and algorithmic adjustments tailored to diverse clinical scenarios \citep{ref_10_1016_j_neuroimage_2020_117203,ref_10_1016_j_arr_2024_102497}. In conclusion, while the potential of AI in revolutionizing the early diagnosis and management of Alzheimer's disease is evident, ongoing research must address critical gaps in data diversity, model validation, and clinical applicability to realize this promise effectively.
